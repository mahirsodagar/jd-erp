# 11 — Audit & Reports

Two audiences: **faculty and staff who file reports**, and the **audit team who
review them**. Menu groups: **Dashboard** (for filing your own) and **Audit**
(for reviewing everyone's).

---

## 11.1 The split you need to understand

The same information appears in two places, deliberately:

| You want to… | Go to |
|---|---|
| Fill in **your own** daily report | **Dashboard → Daily Report** |
| Fill in **your own** admin daily report | **Dashboard → Admin Daily Report** |
| Fill in **your own** 0-Hour report | **Academics → 0-Hour Form** |
| Read **everyone's** reports | **Audit → …** |

So if you are looking for the form to fill in yourself and can only find a
read-only list, you are on the audit side of the menu. Filing your own is under
Dashboard or Academics.

# Part A — Reports you file

## 11.2 Faculty Daily Report

**Dashboard → Daily Report.** One entry per day, laid out as a monthly grid.

For each day you record three pairs of description and hours:

| Category | For |
|---|---|
| **Academic** | Classes taught, preparation, academic documentation |
| **Non-academic** | Committee work, mentoring, administrative tasks |
| **Others** | Anything else |

Fill it in daily rather than in a batch at month end — the audit team compares
what you logged against what was scheduled on your timetable.

## 11.3 Admin Daily Report

**Dashboard → Admin Daily Report.** For administrative and HR staff.

Two free-text slots per day:

- the **morning slot** (roughly 9:30–12:30)
- the **afternoon slot** (roughly 1:30–5:30)

Also a monthly grid. One entry per person per day.

## 11.4 Course-End Report

**Audit → Course-End Reports.** Filed by the instructor when they finish
teaching a subject to a batch.

You record:

- a **summary** of how the course went
- **learning outcomes** achieved
- **challenges** faced
- **suggestions** for next time
- **average attendance** and **average marks** for the batch

One report per instructor, subject and batch.

**The HOD then reviews it** and either **approves** it or **returns it for
rework** with remarks. Returned means read the remarks, revise and resubmit —
it is not a rejection.

## 11.5 Batch-Mentor Report

**Audit → Batch-Mentor Reports.** Filed monthly by the batch mentor. One per
batch per month.

You record:

- **average attendance** and **average marks** for the month
- **behavioural notes**
- **academic concerns**
- **dropout risks** — students you are worried about
- **initiatives** you have taken
- any **additional remarks**

The dropout-risk field is the one management reads most closely. Be specific
and name students where it matters.

## 11.6 Self-Appraisal

**Audit → Faculty Feedback.** Filed quarterly. One per person per quarter.

| Quarter | Period |
|---|---|
| Q1 | April – June |
| Q2 | July – September |
| Q3 | October – December |
| Q4 | January – March |

You record your **achievements**, **challenges**, **plans**, plus **green
flags** (notable positives — mentorship, initiatives, results) and **red
flags** (areas needing improvement).

Being candid in the red-flags section is the point of the exercise. The auditor
adds their remarks, and the four quarters together feed the yearly appraisal.

You can see your own; seeing everyone's is a separate permission.

# Part B — Reviewing (the audit team)

## 11.7 Audit Forms — building your own forms

**Audit → Audit Forms.**

Design a form once, publish it to whichever roles should fill it, and collect
the responses.

### Building a form

1. **Create** it — a title and description. It starts as a **draft**, visible
   only to you.
2. **Add fields.** Available types: short text, long text, radio buttons,
   dropdown, multi-select, checkboxes, star rating, date, time, and date-and-
   time. For each field set the label, whether it is required, any help text,
   and the options where relevant. Order them as you want them to appear.
3. **Choose the target roles.** Only people holding one of those roles will see
   the form.
4. **Publish** it. It now appears under **Forms to fill** for everyone in those
   roles.
5. **Close** it when you have enough responses.

Publishing is a separate permission from editing the questions, because
publishing pushes the form to a whole role's worth of staff at once.

A form can only be **deleted while it has no responses**. Once people have
answered, close it rather than deleting it.

### Reading responses

**Audit → Filled Forms** lists every submission with who sent it and when. Open
one to read the answers.

> Note: the permission to *view forms* on the audit side is a manager-level
> permission — it lets you see **every** form, including drafts and ones aimed
> at other roles. Everyone else only reaches published forms addressed to their
> own role.

## 11.8 Reviewing the daily reports

**Audit → Faculty Daily Report** and **Audit → Admin Daily Report** show
everyone's entries in the same monthly grid format.

Alongside the faculty grid there is a **computed view** that derives, from the
timetable and leave records, how many class hours each person was actually
scheduled for and how many hours they were on leave — so you can compare what
was logged against what was expected.

Note that it skips Sundays and does **not** deduct public holidays.

## 11.9 Student Feedback

**Audit → Student Feedback.**

Students give mid-course and end-of-course feedback: an overall rating plus
clarity, engagement and responsiveness, and free-text on what worked and what
could improve.

**Feedback is anonymous to the instructor.** Instructors see only aggregated
ratings for themselves.

> The permission to view student feedback on this page is what **removes that
> anonymity** — it shows the student's name against their comments. Grant it
> sparingly and treat what you see accordingly. It is a privacy decision, not a
> convenience setting.

## 11.10 Flags & Stars

**Audit → Flags & Stars.** One page, two opposite purposes.

### 🚩 Flags — concerns

A flag records something that went wrong: a missed deadline, low attendance, an
unsubmitted report, a negative feedback trend, an academic concern.

Each has a **severity** — low, medium or high — and a description, and can be
raised against a faculty member, a batch, a student, or just described in text.

Flags are **resolvable**: once the issue is addressed, resolve it with remarks.
Resolved flags stay on record.

### ⭐ Stars — recognition

A star records something that went right: outstanding performance, timely
completion, excellent student feedback, innovation, or another achievement.

Each carries a **1-to-5 rating**.

**Stars are permanent and are never resolved** — they are a lasting record of
good work, not an open item.

One permission covers raising both — recognising good work and recording a
concern are treated as the same responsibility. Correcting or removing a flag
or star raised in error needs its own permission.

## 11.11 0-Hour Reports

**Audit → 0-Hour Reports.** Read-only review of every 0-Hour session report,
organised by date. Mentors file theirs under **Academics → 0-Hour Form** —
see [chapter 7](07-academics-records.md) §7.4.

## 11.12 Dashboards

**Audit → Dashboards.** Five views, each behind its own permission.

| Dashboard | Shows |
|---|---|
| **Live faculty tracking** | For every active faculty member today: their teaching load, their attendance status, and whether they have submitted their daily report |
| **Timetable adherence** | Over a chosen period, how many scheduled classes actually had attendance marked — the working measure of whether classes are happening |
| **Batch progression** | For one batch: enrolment count, average attendance, how much of the marks have been published, and certification status |
| **Instructor feedback summary** | Aggregated student ratings for one named instructor |
| **Consolidated monthly** | Cross-institute headline numbers for a month |

Live faculty tracking and the instructor feedback summary each need their own
permission, because they are views of a **named colleague's** activity rather
than institute-wide statistics.

> **Reading timetable adherence correctly.** A class counts as delivered when
> its attendance was marked. A class that was taught but where nobody marked
> the register looks undelivered. Before treating a low figure as a teaching
> problem, check whether it is a marking problem.

## 11.13 Filters

The audit pages have their own filter lookups for campuses, programs, batches
and employees, so you can filter freely without needing access to the Master
Data screens.

## 11.14 What your actions affect

| When you… | This happens elsewhere |
|---|---|
| Submit your daily report | It appears in the audit grid and in live faculty tracking |
| Publish an audit form | Everyone in the targeted roles sees it under Forms to fill |
| Raise a flag | It goes on that person's or batch's record until resolved |
| Award a star | It goes on the record permanently |
| Return a course-end report | It goes back to the instructor for revision |
| Record auditor remarks on a self-appraisal | The employee sees them on their own appraisal |
